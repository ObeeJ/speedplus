Four things you need. One way to receive them.

An identity built on the moment that actually matters in this business: the handover. Not the ride, not the app, not the speed. The second something protected opens and reaches the person who paid for it.

01Three routes

Three directions were drawn. One is recommended.

Each starts from a different truth about Fourdat. The brief asks for real alternatives rather than one predictable answer, so these are genuinely different arguments, not three finishes of the same idea.

Recommended · 01

The Aperture

A closed, protective form with a single opening cut through it. The opening is divided into four channels that read as one line. Four verticals, one route in.

Carries protection and release in the same shape, which is what Fourdat actually sells. The four is present without being counted.

Alternate · 02

The Handover

Two hooks, identical, rotated into each other. Neither closes alone. The channel between them is the exchange itself.

Warmer and more human, but it reads as a logistics mark first. It also gets fragile below 20px, where the channel fills in.

Alternate · 03

The Seal

A soft square with one corner turned back, like a sealed parcel or a signed document. The fold is where the second colour lives.

The most immediately readable and the easiest to produce, but closest to what a courier brand would already own.

Direction 01 is developed in full below. The rest of this document is that system.

02The idea

An opening in something that was closed.

Fourdat's real promise is not speed. It is that money and goods stay held until the right person has them. Your own positioning already says it plainly: your money doesn't move until your order does.

So the mark is a container, not an arrow. A full, confident form with weight to it, cut once by a diagonal opening. The opening runs upward from left to right, so the whole shape leans forward without ever becoming a delivery chevron.

That opening is broken into four segments. At the sizes people actually see a brand, they merge into one clean channel. Look closer and the four is there. It is a fact about the company, not a diagram of it, which means Fourdat can add a fifth service without redrawing anything.

What it borrows from

The silhouette of a cowrie shell, opened along its aperture. Cowries were West African currency for centuries. They are the oldest visual shorthand on this continent for value that can be trusted, counted and handed over. Nobody has to know that for the mark to work. It is simply why it feels like it comes from somewhere.

03Wordmark

FOURDAT is drawn, not set.

The letters are built on one grid: a 100-unit cap height, a 20-unit stem, and a 14-unit gap that never changes. Nothing here is a licensed font with the tracking pulled in.

Flat-apex A

A pointed A adds a spike to a name that should feel level. The apex is cut flat and the stems splay slightly. It is the letter people will remember without knowing why.

Squircle O and D

Both bowls use the same corner logic as the symbol. That is the join between mark and name, so the two never look like they were made by different people.

Even colour

Monolinear weight across all seven letters, with no optical thinning at the joins. It stays solid embroidered, screen-printed, or cut in vinyl at three metres.

Expressive cut

For covers, brand films, murals and the app splash, the O is replaced by the symbol itself. Use it once in a piece, never in navigation, never in running text.

05Colour

Deep green carries it. Warmth answers it. The bright colour is a status, not a brand.

The product already runs on a deep green with an acid lime. That green is right and it stays. The lime is the part causing the trouble, so it is demoted: from today it means live, and nothing else. A rider is moving, an order is being tracked, a payment released. It never appears on a logo, a business card or a shopfront.

In its place the brand gains Clay, a laterite red drawn from the earth these bikes actually ride on. It is the warmth the identity was missing, and per your brief it stays an accent. It never runs the page.

07Sub-brands

The master brand never changes. Only the chip does.

Each vertical gets a colour and a glyph. The symbol stays Palm, the wordmark stays as drawn, and the vertical name is set in Bricolage next to it. A fifth service is a new chip, not a new identity.

Naming rule

Always Fourdat + the plain English noun. Never Fourdat Rx, never FourdatGo, never a compound with the four in it. The master brand carries the trust; the second word only has to say what is in the bag.

09Rules

What keeps it recognisable.

Do
  • Let Palm hold large areas. The brand is confident when it commits to a full field of green.
  • Switch to the compact symbol at 24px and below, in code, automatically.
  • Keep Signal for live states, and only ever one Signal element per view.
  • Set delivery and tracking codes in Martian Mono, everywhere, without exception.
Don't
  • Rotate the symbol. The aperture's angle is the only angle it has.
  • Add a gradient, a bevel, an outer glow or a drop shadow to the mark itself.
  • Re-set FOURDAT in a font. The wordmark ships as artwork and is used as artwork.
  • Put the four verticals inside the logo. They live in chips, never in the mark.
